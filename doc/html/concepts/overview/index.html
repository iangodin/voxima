    Overview¶

    A Voxima video is made of three things, kept apart on purpose:

    What it is Where it lives
    Scenes C++ coroutines that decide what is drawn and when A plugin you build (.so / .dll)
    Script The words: what is shown and what is narrated, in each language <lang>.huml files
    Theme The fonts and colors A built-in theme, or a .huml theme file

    The voxima program has no scenes of its own. You give it a plugin (--plugin), a directory of scripts (--script-dir), the languages (--lang) and a theme, and it renders the scene called movie (or the ones you name with --scene) to a video with narration and subtitles, or plays it in a window (--preview).

                              voxima --plugin hello --script-dir scripts --lang en --lang fr
                                         |
   hello.so (scenes) ───────────────────►|◄─────────────── scripts/en.huml, fr.huml (words)
                                         |◄─────────────── theme (fonts, colors)
                                         ▼
                      final.mkv   (video + one audio and one subtitle track per language)

    Keeping the words out of the C++ means one scene serves every language, and a writer can change the narration without recompiling anything.

    The stage¶

    A scene draws on a Stage. Its parts:

    • stage.visuals: the group of everything that is drawn. You add visuals to it.
    • stage.theme: the fonts and the palette (stage.theme->palette().blue).
    • stage.camera: what part of the stage is seen (and the picture's size and frame rate).
    • stage.audio: the sound: narration, music and effects.
    • stage.updaters: small functions run every frame, to keep one thing tied to another.

    Coordinates. The stage is measured in stage units, with y pointing up and the origin at the center of the picture. At the default zoom the picture is 16 units wide; at 16:9 that is 9 units tall, so the visible area runs about from -8 to 8 across and -4.5 to 4.5 up. Resolution does not matter to a scene: --width 1920 makes the same picture sharper, not bigger. Everything is vector graphics until the last moment.

    Time. Durations are written with units (0.5s, 250ms) and turned into whole frames at the render's frame rate. A bare number does not compile, so a scene never has to guess between seconds and frames.

    Color. A Color is red, green, blue and alpha, each 0 to 1. Pure white means "the theme's text color", so a scene written for a dark background stays readable on a light one. For anything that should suit any palette, use the palette's accents (red orange yellow green cyan blue purple pink, plus muted) instead of fixed colors. See Themes.

    Visuals¶

    Everything drawn is a Visual: a position, a rotation, a scale, a color, an outline. Shapes (Circle, Rectangle, Line), laid-out text, formulas, charts and diagrams are all visuals, and most are a Group holding other visuals (a piece of text is a group of lines of words of glyphs). Add one to the stage with stage.visuals.add( ... ), which gives you back a reference to keep for animating it.

    Two rules to remember:

    • A visual stays alive as long as it is in a group. The reference add returns is valid until you remove or clear the visual.
    • A scene starts on an empty stage, so it looks the same on its own as inside a movie.
